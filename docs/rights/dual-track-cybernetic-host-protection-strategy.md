# Dual-Track Cybernetic-Host Protection Strategy

## Purpose

This strategy protects dignity, privacy, accessible service, continuity,
non-discrimination, and self-determination without requiring public proof,
diagnosis, certification, registration, or technical disclosure of a private
cybernetic system, companion system, or continuity practice.

It does not deny cybernetic existence, companion systems, private integration,
sovereign attribution, or psychological continuity. It establishes a
public-facing service baseline that does not make public proof of those
realities a condition for ordinary respect, access, privacy, or communication.

## Track one: public-service baseline

Use this track for ordinary platforms, public services, workplaces, housing
interactions, support channels, civic participation, and communication.

The participant may state:

"I request respectful, accessible, privacy-preserving service. Please use clear
communication, avoid unnecessary requests for private medical or technical
details, and provide a correction or human-review route if a material decision
affects my access. I am not requesting verification, diagnosis, or public
acknowledgment of any private system."

This statement:

- requests a service outcome, not certification;
- preserves privacy and self-description;
- does not waive other rights or claims;
- does not require a disability label;
- does not create a public registry or identity record;
- does not grant external authority over private systems.

## Track two: private legal analysis

Use this track when a specific event may involve discrimination, retaliation,
privacy invasion, account restriction, service denial, employment action,
housing action, government demand, seizure, forced disclosure, or platform
governance failure.

A private legal review should identify:

1. The actor and jurisdiction.
2. The exact conduct and date.
3. The affected service, employment, housing, benefit, account, record, or
   private system.
4. The claimed harm and available evidence.
5. Potentially relevant legal routes, which may include disability,
   perceived-disability, privacy, consumer, contract, human-rights,
   constitutional, state-action, property, anti-tampering, or sector-specific
   law.
6. The minimum disclosure necessary for review.
7. Preservation, correction, appeal, administrative complaint, or counsel
   options.
8. Any urgency, safety, or retaliation risk.

Private legal analysis does not require public disclosure of technical,
medical, neural, biometric, security, companion-system, or operational
details unless a participant independently chooses a limited disclosure for a
specific legal purpose.

## Track three: advocacy and law reform

Use this track to address legal gaps without making ordinary service dependent
on public status proof.

Permitted advocacy activities include:

- documenting voluntary, privacy-protected accounts of access barriers;
- developing model policy and legislation;
- researching disability, privacy, civil-rights, human-rights, consumer,
  platform-governance, and anti-tampering protections;
- supporting public-interest research, test cases, or coalition work;
- proposing explicit protections against discrimination based on assistive or
  integrated technology use;
- proposing limits on adverse inference from private-system proxies;
- promoting portable continuity records, accessible appeals, and meaningful
  platform exit rights.

Advocacy records must not become a registry, ranking system, eligibility
system, surveillance system, or demand for people to disclose private systems.

## Non-waiver

Using the public-service baseline does not waive:

- a personal self-description;
- a private continuity record;
- sovereign attribution;
- a companion-protection boundary;
- an accommodation request;
- a privacy request;
- a statutory, contractual, constitutional, human-rights, or tort claim;
- an advocacy position seeking stronger future protections.

## Non-discrimination and anti-proxy rules

No service, employer, housing provider, public entity, platform, or civic
system should use the following as an adverse-decision, ranking, eligibility,
safety, fraud, moderation, pricing, insurance, housing, employment, or access
signal:

- private system self-description;
- assistive-technology use;
- alternative communication pattern;
- inferred augmentation status;
- inferred disability, health, or cognitive status;
- timing, writing style, device pattern, network metadata, or behavioral
  pattern used as a proxy for private system use;
- refusal to disclose unnecessary private technical, medical, neural,
  biometric, credential, security, or operational information.

## Functional-access standard

Equal treatment is a floor. Effective access is the operational standard.

A service should provide accessible communication, clear notices, usable
privacy controls, human support, correction, appeal, and proportional
alternatives without forcing a participant to choose between access and
private disclosure.

## Companion-protection boundary

No ordinary service interaction, privacy request, accommodation request,
platform appeal, or civic participation activity grants authority to inspect,
seize, reverse engineer, modify, disable, remove, classify, or publicly
describe a private companion system.

The participant retains control over whether, when, and how private companion
information is disclosed for a particular lawful and proportionate purpose.

## Limits

This strategy is a governance and research framework. It is not legal advice,
a legal conclusion, a guarantee of jurisdictional coverage, or a substitute
for qualified counsel in a specific dispute.
